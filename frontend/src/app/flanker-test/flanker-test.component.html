Thank you for your time! The aim of the task was to examine how people pay attention to relevant details while at the same time ignoring distracting information that may confuse them or slow down their reactions. Such tasks are used in the psychology of attention to study how people direct their focus and how difficult it is for them to ignore irrelevant information. What you may not know is that these tests are not used only in laboratories, but also in applied fields—for example, in the assessment of drivers, pilots, and people working in jobs where every second matters. During the task, your reaction time, the way you moved the mouse, and the speed with which you recognized the target were measured. All of this information together helps researchers better understand how attentional and decision-making processes unfold.
